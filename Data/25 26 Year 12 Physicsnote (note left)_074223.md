**Einstein Photoelectric Equation**

Einstein’s photo-electric equation is given by;

E = W0 + K.E

W0 = hf0 = h$\frac{c}{λ0}$

Where f0 = threshold frequency,$ $ $λ0=tℎresℎold wavelengtℎ$

E = hf = h$\frac{c}{λ}$

KE = ½ mv2 = eV

hf = hf0 + ½ mv2

Where **E = hf** is the energy of the incident radiation/photon, **W0**= work function of the metal (i.e energy used to remove electron from the surface of a metal), h = Planck constant, K.E = Maximum Kinetic energy/energy of photoelectrons.

**Threshold frequency (f0):** This is the minimum frequency required to liberate electrons from the surface of a metal.

**Work function (W0)**: This is the minimum work/energy required to liberate/eject electrons from the surface of a metal when irradiated by electromagnetic radiation.

The equation represents the principle of conservation of energy.

**Stopping Potential**

K.E = E – W0

eV = hf – hf0

V = $\frac{ℎf}{e }$ - $\frac{hf0}{e }$ = $\frac{hf−W0}{e }$ = $\frac{eV}{e }$ (IeV = 1.6 x 10-19 J) where V is stopping potential.

**Stopping potential (V):** This is the negative potential of the anode of a photocell that would just prevent electrons from reaching the anode.

**Conditions for Photo-electrons Emission**

1. The energy of the incident photons radiation (E) must be **greater than** the work function (W0) of the metal (E>W0).
2. The wavelength of incident radiation (λ) must be **less than** the threshold wavelength (λ0) for metal surface. (λ < λ0)
3. The frequency of incident radiation (f) must be **greater than** the threshold frequency (f0). (f > f0)

**Note:**

* The maximum kinetic energy or maximum velocity of the emitted electron/photoelectrons is independent of the intensity of the incident light (photon) and is proportional to the frequency or wavelength.
* There exists a threshold frequency for all surface and below this frequency, no electrons are emitted, no matter the intensity of radiation.
* The number of photoelectrons emitted per second is directly proportional to the intensity of radiation.
* For energy of light with frequency less than the threshold frequency, complete absorption takes place. The energy absorbed is used to raise the energy of electrons or overcome the potential barrier in the photoelectric material.

**Hardness and Intensity of X-rays**

**Soft X-ray:** x-ray with longer wavelength and lower frequency/lower penetrating power.

11. **Postulates:**
12. That the electron moves around the nucleus in certain specific circular orbits (also called ***energy levels***) and that the centrifugal force due to this motion counterbalances the electrostatic attraction between the electron and the nucleus.

In such orbits the electrons can move without radiating or losing energy. He called the possible orbits ***stationary states.*** In general, the higher the energy of the electron, the farther its orbit from the nucleus.

1. That the energy of an electron in an atom cannot vary continuously but is restricted to a limited number of ***discrete or individual values***. The energy of the electron is said to be ***quantized (i.e can have only discrete values).***

Bohr thus postulated that the electrons in an atom cannot lose energy continuously but must do so in quantum ‘Jumps’.

He also postulated that light is emitted only when an electron jumps from one stationary state to another of lower energy. When such a jump occurs, a single photon of light would be emitted whose energy is given by;

**ΔE = hf = Eu – EL**

Where E = Energy of photon or Quantum, Eu = Energy of upper state, EL= Energy of Lower state, f = frequency of the emitted light, h = Planck’s constant = 6.626 x 10-34Js.

Similarly, when an electron moves from a lower energy level to a higher energy level, it absorbs energy. This is called **excitation.**

Bohr was able to account for the appearance of ***line spectrum*** rather than ***continuous spectrum.***
