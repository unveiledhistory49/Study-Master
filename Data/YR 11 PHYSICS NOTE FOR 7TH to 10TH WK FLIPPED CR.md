Dispersion of white light is the separation/splitting of white light into
| seven | component | colours/wavelength |     | when passing | through | a   |
| ----- | --------- | ------------------ | --- | ------------ | ------- | --- |
triangular prism. The seven colours which are ROYGBIV and stands for
Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo and Violet are known as the
| spectrum | of white | light. |     |     |     |     |
| -------- | -------- | ------ | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Dispersion occurs because refraction or bending differs with the colour.
Different colours of white light travels at different speeds through the
glass prism and have different frequencies. Each colour is therefore,
refracted in a slightly different direction or angle through the glass.
Red Light is deviated least (i.e. bend the least) and the violet light is
deviated most (i.e. bend the most). Since red is deviated least, its
change of speed on entering the glass prism is least. Therefore, red light
travels in glass prism with the greatest speed. Violet light travels in
| glass prism | with | the least speed. |     |     |     |
| ----------- | ---- | ---------------- | --- | --- | --- |
Note:
| i.In vacuum, |     | all the colours | travel | at the same | speed. |
| ------------ | --- | --------------- | ------ | ----------- | ------ |
ii. Violet bend the most and red light bend the least. This is
because violet light has the shortest wavelength. The shorter the
wavelength, the slower the light will travel. So violet light travel with
the least speed through the prism. Red light travels with the greatest
| speed           | because | it has the            | longest | wavelength. |     |
| --------------- | ------- | --------------------- | ------- | ----------- | --- |

Monochromatic light: This is the light that has one wavelength (i.e. one
colour). When this light is passed through a prism, refraction occurs
without dispersion. Exampe: Yellow light obtained from a sodium lamp.
Pure Spectrum: This is a spectrum in which the colours are clearly
separated or distinct from one another. This can be produced using a
triangular prism, two converging lenses and a narrow slit of white light.
Impure Spectrum: This is a spectrum in which different colours overlap.

| Production |     | of Sound |                                             | Waves |     |     |     |     |     |
Sound is a form of energy which produces the sensation of hearing in
our ears. Sound wave is produced by vibration of an object. These
vibrations cause the surrounding air to vibrate. Sound waves are
propagated through air by the displacement of air molecules. They travel
in form of longitudinal waves. The molecules vibrate in a direction
parallel to the direction of travel of the waves (Longitudinal wave).

| Transmission |     |     | of Sound |     | Waves |     |     |     |
| ------------ | --- | --- | -------- | --- | ----- | --- | --- | --- |
Sound can be transmitted through air, gases, solids and liquids. The
speed of sound transmitted depends on the state of matter (slower
through gases, faster through liquid and fastest through solids). Sound
does not readily pass from one media to another due to the difference in
densities of the respective media. This is why we do not readily hear our
heart beat, except by pressing the ear to the chest or by using a
stethoscope which provides a solid connection between the ear and the
chest.

ii. Refraction of Sound Waves: Sound waves are refracted when
they pass from one medium to another of slightly different
density. This is why we hear sound coming from a distance
easily at night than in the day. At night the layers of air near the
earth are colder than those higher up. Sound waves are
therefore, refracted towards the earth and this increases the
intensity of sound.
iii. Diffraction of Sound Waves: Sound waves have a much longer
wavelength than light. They can even diffract around buildings
or through doorways. This is why we can often hear people
when we can’t see them.
iv. Interference of Sound Waves: When two sound waves interfere
constructively, a loud sound is produced because sound from
both sources reinforces each other. When they interfere
destructively, silence or no sound is observed because the crest

of one source overlaps the trough of equal amplitude from the
other source.
v. Polarization of Sound Waves: Sound waves do not undergo
polarization because it is a longitudinal wave. Polarization is
exclusive to transverse waves.
Characteristics of Sound Waves
1. Pitch or Shrillness: This enables us to differentiate a high note
from a low note. Pitch of a sound depends on frequency (Pitch α
Frequency).
2. Loudness and Intensity: Intensity of a sound is the rate of flow
of energy per second per unit area perpendicular to the direction
of propagation of the sound wave.
Loudness is the magnitude of the sensation resulting from a
sound reaching the ear. Loudness depends on intensity of the
sound, the square of amplitude of the sound, the mass of the air
which can be set into vibration. The unit of loudness is the
decibel (dB).
A soft sound has small amplitude, but a loud sound as high
amplitude. The greater the mass of air set into vibration, the
louder the sound produced. This is the principle of sounding
board of a piano or the diaphragm of a loud speaker.
3. Quality or Time (Timbre): Quality distinguishes one note of an
instrument from another note of the same pitch and loudness
produced by another instrument. The quality of a note depends
on the overtones or Harmonics present in the note.
Noise and Music

an unpleasant mixture of sounds. E.g. sound produced by

frequencies which are in the ratio 2:1, the two notes are said to
differ by an octave.
